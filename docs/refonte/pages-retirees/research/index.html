Research

PharmD, PhD student in pharmacology. I work on model-informed precision dosing: mechanistic pharmacokinetic models fitted to an individual patient's measured concentrations, used to support a dosing decision. Version française.

The question

Two patients given the same dose of the same drug do not reach the same blood concentration. A mathematical model of what the body does to the drug can be fitted to the handful of concentrations measured in one patient, and then used to propose a dose for that patient. This is routine clinical practice in transplantation, intensive care and oncology.

My published work addresses that individualisation: how residual error is handled when individual exposures are computed, and dose optimisation of antifungals in critically ill patients. It is listed below, with identifiers.

An equation that changes no prescription has produced nothing.

My doctoral work sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence and pharmacometrics, and aims at digital twins. It is ongoing and is not detailed here.

Where this work sits

I carry out this doctoral work at INSERM UMR 1248 "Pharmacology and Transplantation", a joint research unit of the University of Limoges, INSERM and Limoges University Hospital, in Limoges, France. My thesis is supervised by Prof. Jean-Baptiste Woillard, who is also deputy director of the unit; the unit itself is directed by Prof. Pierre Marquet. Its stated areas include pharmacokinetic modelling and personalised medicine, and AI-augmented pharmacology; it develops and maintains the expert system for Bayesian dose adaptation of immunosuppressants used by transplant centres.

I am a member of the DIGPHAT consortium on digital pharmacological twins, which brings together around ten French institutions across three use cases: transplantation, infectious diseases and oncology. The consortium's conceptual framework is set out in Woillard JB et al., Therapie, 2026 — doi.org/10.1016/j.therap.2025.09.006.
